Sync between your devices

October 2026 status. The sync core is implemented and unit-tested (merging, clocks, replay — see For developers), and both wire transports are validated: the nostr spool round-trips against a live public relay, and the iroh stream built and ran on Android hardware in a spike. The in-app Sync screens you tap through (QR pairing, the Sync now button) are the current work item. This page documents what the design guarantees and what the UI will walk you through; nothing here already syncs on its own.

How it works, in three sentences

  1. Your review log is the only shared truth: a grow-only set of review events, each stamped with a globally unique id (UUID) and a hybrid logical clock. Devices merge logs by deduplicating on that id — the union of both sets, no matter the arrival order.
  2. Memory states are never synced. Each device re-computes its FSRS schedule locally by deterministically re-folding the merged log (per card, ordered by clock then id) — so any set of devices that has seen the same events converges to byte-identical schedules, and divergent floating-point scheduler state can never need merging.
  3. Settings merge last-write-wins per key under the logical clock, with one extra rule — absence is not a value — so the alphabet-gate latch (pass the check on your desktop, phone unlocks) can never be lost by syncing from an older device.

These are CRDT properties (convergence, idempotency, commutativity), and each one maps to a test in the repo. Full specification: DESIGN-device-sync.md.

Two transports — pick either or both

1. Direct pairing (iroh)

For two devices that can be online at the same time. Sync is peer-to-peer (QUIC), relay-assisted only for NAT traversal — no server ever stores your data. Pairing is one gesture:

  1. On the device that has your progress: Settings → Sync → Show QR. The QR encodes an iroh-docs share ticket (greeksync://ticket/…) — treat it as a credential.
  2. On the other device: Join from code, scan the QR or paste the ticket string.
  3. Both devices sync immediately and hereafter whenever both are online together (plus a manual Sync now button).

The QR contains the write capability for the shared sync document. Show it to someone else's camera, and that's their access. Never send it over a network.

2. Offline store-and-forward (nostr spool)

For devices that are never online together: each device encrypts its sync batches to itself (NIP-44) and publishes them to a nostr relay as ordinary events (kind 1078, chunked at 48 KiB, with a kind-30078 checkpoint whose tag is HMAC-blinded). The relay is untrusted storage — it serves ciphertext to whichever of your devices asks next.

  1. Settings → Sync, enter the relay URL.
  2. Enter or generate your sync key (nsec). The same key on all your devices is the whole identity system.
  3. That's it — devices push and pull through the relay asynchronously.
The default relay belongs to the developer. If your build ships with a prefilled URL (wss://chat.virginiafreedom.tech), that's the developer's private Pyramid relay, listed for the developer's own convenience — do not use it for your data. Point the field at any relay you control or trust: your own strfry/nostr-rs-relay instance is the intended production setup, and public relays also work. Verified-working public options include wss://relay.damus.io, wss://nos.lol, and wss://offchain.pub. Public relays rate-limit fresh keys and bursts (measured: damus rejects a ~140-event burst from a new key), so the transport paces chunk publishes (500 ms apart, 10 s backoff on rate-limited) — a large first sync takes minutes on a public relay, seconds on a private one. Details: SYNC-WIRE.md §2.

Phone ↔ desktop pairing Status: rolling out

The desktop companion is in development — nothing in this section has shipped yet. This is the exact flow being built in Wave 7, on top of the sync core and transports above (which exist and are tested).

  1. On the device that already has your progress, open its Sync card and show the pairing ticket: on the desktop it's a copyable ticket string; on the phone the same ticket is shown as a QR code. The ticket carries the write capability for the shared sync document — treat it like a credential and never send it over a network.
  2. On the other device: paste the ticket (or scan the QR) and press Join. Pairing and authorization happen in that one gesture.
  3. Press Sync now for an immediate merge; thereafter the devices merge whenever they're online together.

Never have both devices online at once? Take the nostr relay path instead: point both devices at your own relay — or any relay you trust — and they exchange store-and-forward batches asynchronously, as described above.

Wire hygiene: only ciphertext crosses the wire. Direct pairing runs peer-to-peer over QUIC/TLS, and relay traffic is NIP-44 self-encrypted — no plaintext review history or settings ever touch a relay or a middlebox.

Backup / export — "transport zero"

The same merge layer doubles as disaster recovery: a third transport writes your entire syncable state — full review log with ids, devices, clocks, plus all settings and the paired-device roster — to one JSON file. Importing it on a fresh install replays the log and rebuilds memory states, and sync picks up from there. It needs no network and no paired device, so even if you never enable sync, you have an off-device copy of months of study history.

Privacy of synced data

Everything above is designed around an untrusted middle:

  • nostr payloads are NIP-44 self-encrypted; the private relay runbook write-whitelists a single public key so the relay can't host anyone else's data.
  • Checkpoint tags are HMAC-blinded so the relay can't even enumerate sync metadata by label.
  • Malformed, forged, or replayed events are silently dropped — signature-checked against your own key before decrypting.
  • Secrets live in Android Keystore-backed encrypted storage; a lost device is dropped from the roster and you rotate the key/ticket on the survivors.

See Privacy for what a relay could and could not infer.
